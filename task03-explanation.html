Traffic Accidents in Barcelona

Task 1 · Part 4 — Selection of charts, visual encoding, Gestalt principles and low-fi dashboard draft

Presentation and Visualization

Dataset basis and dashboard goal

Dataset basis. This proposal uses the field dictionary in campos_dataset_accidentes.xlsx. The workbook describes the available fields but does not contain the actual accident rows, so the charts below are design proposals; no counts, trends, rankings or hotspots are claimed as observed results.

Dashboard goal

Explore when and where recorded traffic accidents occur in Barcelona, and describe their recorded outcomes. The dashboard is descriptive: accident counts alone do not establish the probability of an accident, causal explanations, or how people perceive risk.

Proposed questions the dashboard should answer

  • When are recorded accidents most frequent by day of week and hour?
  • How are recorded accidents distributed across Barcelona districts?
  • How do recorded casualty outcomes (fatalities, serious injuries, minor injuries and no victims) vary by district or time?
  • Where are accident records geographically concentrated, where valid coordinates are available?

1. Charts and visual encoding

Chart 1 — Heatmap: accidents by weekday and hour

Goal. Identify temporal concentrations in recorded accidents and support comparisons between weekdays and hours.

Aggregation and selection. Count distinct numero_expedient records for each combination of descripcio_dia_setmana and hora_dia. Use hour as whole-number bins from 0–23 and order weekdays Monday–Sunday. Exclude or separately flag missing/invalid dates or hours; check for duplicate case IDs before counting. A count measures recorded incidents, not risk per trip or hour of exposure.

Chart 2 — Horizontal bar chart: recorded accidents by district

Goal. Compare the number of recorded accident cases across the 10 districts and identify which districts have the highest counts in the selected period.

Aggregation and selection. Count distinct numero_expedient by nom_districte; sort bars descending. Treat “Desconegut” or invalid district codes (the dictionary notes possible 0/−1 codes) as unknown rather than a real district. Add a date-range filter. If district populations, traffic volume or exposure are not available, label the metric “recorded accidents” and do not interpret counts as comparative risk.

Chart 3 — Stacked bar chart: recorded severity by district

Goal. Compare the composition of recorded outcomes across districts, not just total accident counts.

Aggregation and selection. Count distinct accident records by nom_districte and derived gravedad (documented values include Mortal, Greu, Lleu and Sense víctimes). Verify the derivation and mapping of severity against the raw casualty fields before publishing. Use absolute counts to show workload; optionally use 100% stacked bars to compare composition, clearly labeling percentages. Do not mix incident counts and person counts in the same measure.

Chart 4 — Point / density map: geographic concentration of records

Goal. Show where accident records are geographically concentrated, where valid coordinates are available.

Aggregation and selection. Plot one mark per distinct numero_expedient using the latitude/longitude (or projected X/Y) coordinate fields listed in the dictionary. Exclude records with missing or invalid coordinates (e.g. 0 or placeholder values) and report how many were excluded. Because individual points overlap in dense areas, offer a density/hexbin view when zoomed out. The map shows where records exist, not where it is most dangerous; without exposure data (traffic volume, population) no risk claim should be made.

Use of colour in the dashboard

  • Sequential single-hue blue scale (light → dark) for quantitative magnitude: heatmap cells (Chart 1) and map density (Chart 4). Luminance order is intuitive and colour-blind friendly; rainbow palettes are avoided.
  • One neutral colour for simple counts in Chart 2 (bars): length already encodes the value, so extra colour would add noise.
  • Categorical palette with a fixed order for severity in Chart 3, reused for every severity reference in the dashboard (legend, tooltips, KPIs). Proposed: Mortal = dark, Greu = strong, Lleu = medium, Sense víctimes = light/grey, so the order of seriousness also follows luminance.
  • Grey for “unknown / excluded” data, so missing values are not confused with real categories.
  • Colour is never the only channel: positions, labels and order carry the same information (accessibility). Background stays white/light grey to keep contrast.

2. Layout design and Gestalt laws

Gestalt principleHow is it applied in the dashboard?Why?
ProximityFilters are grouped together in the header; each chart sits next to its own title, legend and notes; KPI cards are placed in one row.Elements that are close are perceived as related, so users know which legend and which controls belong to which chart.
SimilarityThe same severity colours are used in Chart 3, the KPI cards and tooltips; all panels share typography, title style and card shape.Same look = same meaning, which reduces learning effort and makes cross-chart comparison easier.
Common region (enclosure)Every chart is placed inside a bordered card/panel; district outlines on the map enclose points.Boundaries separate independent views and make groups obvious without extra labels.
ContinuityCharts are aligned on a grid with common left edges; bars share a baseline and heatmap rows/columns follow straight lines.Aligned elements guide the eye along a natural reading path (left → right, top → bottom).
ClosureAxis lines are minimal; the viewer completes the grid of the heatmap and bar baselines from light gridlines.Less “ink” reduces clutter while the structure is still perceived as complete.
Figure / groundLight grey page background with white chart cards; dark cells and bars stand out against a light ground; the map base layer is muted.Highlights the data (figure) and keeps context in the background.
Connectedness / common fate (interaction)Selecting a district in Chart 2 or on the map highlights it in all other charts (linked brushing).Elements that change together are perceived as linked, supporting exploration across when / where / severity.

Layout logic. Top: title + filters (date range, district, severity). Second row: KPI cards. Main area (reading order): heatmap (when) → district bar chart (where, by district) → severity stacked bars (outcomes) → map (where, geographic). The most general view comes first and details follow (overview first, details on demand).

4. How the dashboard answers the questions

QuestionHow this question will be answered with the dashboard?
When are recorded accidents most frequent by day of week and hour? Chart 1 (heatmap). Darker cells show weekday–hour combinations with more recorded accidents. Rows (weekday) and columns (hour) allow reading both patterns at once. The date-range filter lets the user check whether the pattern holds across periods. Counts are labelled as recorded incidents, not risk.
How are recorded accidents distributed across Barcelona districts? Chart 2 (sorted horizontal bars) compares the number of distinct records per district on a common baseline, and the ranking is visible from the sort order. Chart 4 (map) complements it with the spatial distribution inside and across districts.
How do recorded casualty outcomes vary by district or time? Chart 3 (stacked bars) shows the composition by severity per district (absolute or 100 %). Using the severity filter and selecting cells in Chart 1 re-computes Chart 3 for a given time slot, so outcomes can be explored by district and by time.
Where are accident records geographically concentrated? Chart 4 (point/density map) shows clusters of records using valid coordinates; excluded records are reported in the KPI card. Linked selection with Chart 2 connects hotspots to their district. Concentration of records is not interpreted as higher risk.
Limitations. The dashboard is descriptive. It relies on recorded accidents, requires checking duplicates in numero_expedient, unknown districts (0/−1 / “Desconegut”), the derivation of gravedad and invalid coordinates, and does not include exposure data, so it cannot support claims about probability, causes or perceived risk.
